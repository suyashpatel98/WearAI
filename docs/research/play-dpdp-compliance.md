# What Google Play and India's DPDP Act require of WearAI

Research for ticket #7 (map #1). Researched 2026-11-06.

> **This is research, not legal advice.** It summarises primary-source text as read on 2026-11-06. Before launch, have an Indian privacy lawyer confirm the DPDP points, especially the items marked **Open**.

## Answer in one paragraph

Google Play's rules apply in full now. WearAI must not generate sexual content. It needs an in-app way to report a generated image. It must use the system photo picker and must not hold broad media permissions. It must complete an accurate Data safety form and publish a privacy policy. Account deletion must be offered both in the app and through a web link. The store listing must target 18+ only, with Restrict Minor Access turned on. The app must target API 36. Before the first production release, a new personal developer account must run a closed test with 12 testers for 14 continuous days.

The DPDP Act's substantive duties are **not in force yet**. They start 18 months after the Rules were published, around **13 May 2027**. MeitY proposed bringing that forward, but as of September 2026 no amending notification had been found. WearAI will probably launch near that date, so build to the Act from day one. That means:

- an itemised notice and a separate consent, with withdrawal as easy as giving consent
- the notice available in English or a language of the Eighth Schedule
- users' rights to access, correction and erasure, plus nomination
- a grievance channel that answers within 90 days, and a named contact person
- security safeguards and one-year log retention
- breach notices to affected Users and to the Board within 72 hours
- contracts with every Data Processor
- **no processing of anyone under 18** unless a parent gives verifiable consent

Storing data outside India is currently allowed. No country has been restricted.

## Part A: Google Play

### A1. AI-generated content (Reference Photos → Outfit images)

Source: Play Console Help, *AI-Generated Content* policy, [answer/14094294](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14094294), and the requirements page [answer/13985936](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13985936) (both read 2026-11-06; the pages show no revision date). The policy was announced 25 Oct 2023 and enforced from early 2024.

- **Scope includes WearAI.** The policy covers "Image or video generated by AI based on text, image, or voice prompts" (13985936). Both the Feed and Style Me generate images from image prompts.
- "Developers are responsible for ensuring that their generative AI apps do not generate offensive content." Its examples of content that violates the policy include "AI-generated non-consensual deepfake sexual material", "Generative AI applications primarily intended to be sexually gratifying", and "Content generated to facilitate bullying and harassment" (14094294).
- **Required feature.** "Apps that generate content using AI must contain in-app user reporting or flagging features that allow users to report or flag offensive content to developers without needing to exit the app." Developers should use those reports "to inform content filtering and moderation" (13985936).
- **Safeguards and testing.** The linked *Best Practices to Safeguard AI-Generated Content* page ([answer/16353813](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16353813)) says to test and secure in line with industry standards. It cites Google's SAIF and the OWASP GenAI Red Teaming Guide, and recommends content filters and classifiers on **both inputs and outputs**.

### A2. Sexual content, nudity, and "undressing" apps

Source: *Inappropriate Content* policy, [answer/9878810](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9878810) (read 2026-11-06).

- "We don't allow apps on Google Play that contain or promote sexual content or profanity."
- Examples of prohibited content include "sexually suggestive poses in which the subject is nude, blurred or minimally clothed, and/or where the clothing would not be acceptable in an appropriate public context."
  - **This directly limits what Outfits WearAI may generate.** Lingerie, sheer clothing and similar Items must be filtered out of generation.
- Also prohibited: "apps that claim to undress people or see through clothing, even if labeled as prank or entertainment apps", and "non-consensual sexual content created via deepfake or similar technology".
  - An app that re-dresses photos of real people sits close to this line. WearAI's prompts, its output filters and its store listing should make clear that it only dresses the User in Outfits, never undresses anyone, and only uses the User's own photos.

### A3. Photo picker and media permissions

Source: *Details on Google Play's Photo and Video Permissions policy*, [answer/14115180](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14115180) (read 2026-11-06).

- Apps targeting API 33+ "may only request the `READ_MEDIA_IMAGES` and `READ_MEDIA_VIDEO` permissions if system pickers" cannot serve their core functionality. The usual qualifying apps are gallery and photo-manager apps. "Apps that have custom pickers are not automatically qualified."
- Apps without such a core use case must remove the permissions from the manifest. Full compliance became mandatory on **28 May 2025**, after which apps that don't comply can be removed.
- **What this means for WearAI.** The map's choice of the Android photo picker with up to 20 photos per upload is compliant. Build check: the **merged** release manifest (Expo plugins and libraries included) must contain no `READ_MEDIA_IMAGES`, `READ_MEDIA_VIDEO` or `READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE`. Otherwise the Play Console will ask for a declaration that WearAI would fail.

### A4. Data safety form, privacy policy, prominent disclosure

Sources: *Data safety section*, [answer/10787469](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469); *User Data* policy, [answer/10144311](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311) (both read 2026-11-06).

- **Who must fill it in.** The form is required for every app on a closed, open or production track. Apps that are *only* on internal testing are exempt (10787469), so the form must be done **before the closed test**.
- **Likely declarations for WearAI:**
  - **Photos** (Reference Photos and camera-roll photos; generated images)
  - **Personal info** (name, email address and user ID from Google Sign-In; age or date of birth if collected)
  - **App activity** (Posts saved and dismissed, Style Me conversations as "other user-generated content")
- **Collection vs sharing.** Sending data off the device is "collection". Sending it to a **service provider** that processes it "on behalf of the developer" under the developer's instructions is **not** "sharing" (10787469).
  - So hosted model APIs count as service providers **only if** they don't use the data for their own purposes, such as training. Choose model API tiers and terms that don't train on customer data, or declare the transfer as sharing.
- **Security answers.** The form asks whether data is encrypted in transit, and whether users can request deletion (10787469).
- **Privacy policy (User Data).**
  - Its link must go in the Play Console **and** inside the app.
  - It must be a public, non-geofenced URL, **not a PDF**.
  - It must cover: the developer's contact and privacy contact, the data collected and used, who receives the data, secure handling, and "the developer's data retention and deletion policy".
  - It must agree with the Data safety form (10144311).
- **Third-party AI.** User Data requirements apply to "third-party AI integrations", and the developer stays responsible for limited use, disclosure and consent (10144311).
- **Prominent disclosure and consent.** These are required when a use "may not be within the reasonable expectation of the user" (10144311).
  - The disclosure must be inside the app, shown during normal use, and not only in the privacy policy. Consent must come right after it, by an affirmative action, before collection begins.
  - Sending a User's body photos to a third-party model is the kind of use to disclose up front. One onboarding screen, before the first Reference Photo upload, can meet this and the DPDP notice and consent (B3) at the same time.
- **No sale of personal data.** Use only for "app and service functionality and policy-conforming purposes reasonably expected by the user" (10144311).

### A5. Account and data deletion

Sources: *Understanding Google Play's app account deletion requirements*, [answer/13327111](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111); User Data policy (10144311). Both read 2026-11-06. Enforcement has been possible since 31 May 2024.

- **Two routes are needed:**
  - an **in-app** way to delete the account and its data
  - a **web link** where deletion can be requested without reinstalling the app. The page must name the app or developer as it appears on Play, and the link goes in the Data safety form.
- **Scope of deletion.** Delete the account *and* the user data linked to it, meaning everything declared in Data safety. Ask service providers to delete their copies too. Give users "a reasonably prompt timeline".
- "Temporary account deactivation, disabling, or 'freezing' ... does not qualify as account deletion" (10144311).
- **Retention.** Data may be kept for security, fraud prevention or legal compliance **if disclosed** in the privacy policy. This is where the DPDP one-year log retention (B6) belongs.

### A6. Enforcing 18+

Source: *Target audience and content*, [answer/9867159](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9867159); Play Age Signals API docs, [overview](https://developer.android.com/google/play/age-signals/overview) (terms last modified Oct 2025). Both read 2026-11-06.

- **Declare 18+.** In *Target audience and content*, choose **18 and over as the only target age group**. This keeps WearAI out of the Families Policy, which applies once any target age group includes children.
- **Turn on Restrict Minor Access.** Then check **Restrict Minor Access**. Users Google has determined to be under 18 "will not be able to search for, download or purchase the app" (9867159).
  - Play describes this as **required** only for some categories (dating, matchmaking, real-money gambling) under its Age-Restricted Content and Functionality policy. For WearAI it is optional, but recommended.
- **Play Age Signals API (beta)** returns age bands (0–12, 13–15, 16–17, 18+). Its terms allow use of the signal **only** "to provide age-appropriate content and experiences in compliance with laws". Advertising, profiling and analytics are banned.
  - Signals are live in Brazil (from 17 Mar 2026) and in some US states (Texas and others; [answer/16569691](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16569691)).
  - A global rollout by the end of 2026 has been reported (secondary sources only; not confirmed for India). **Don't rely on it for India at launch.** Add it later as an extra signal if it reaches India.
- **Play doesn't verify age for you.** Pair the Play settings with an in-app age declaration at onboarding (see B5).

### A7. Testing before production (new developer account)

Source: *App testing requirements for new personal developer accounts*, [answer/14151465](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465) (read 2026-11-06; no revision date shown).

- **Who it applies to.** Personal developer accounts created **after 13 Nov 2023**.
- **What the test needs.** A **closed test** with "at least 12 testers ... opted in continuously for the preceding 14 days" when you apply. Testers who opt out before 14 days don't count, and the 14 days must be consecutive. Internal testing doesn't count.
- **Then apply for production access.** The application asks how easy recruiting testers was, how engaged they were, what feedback came in, the target audience, the app's value proposition, expected installs, and what changed after the test. Review usually takes ≤7 days. Approval unlocks Production and Open testing.
- **Organisation accounts are exempt**, but they need a D-U-N-S number and organisation verification. **Decision for the spec:** a personal account (and plan for the 14+ day closed test) or an organisation account.
- The Data safety form, privacy policy and content declarations must be complete before the closed test (A4).

### A8. Other publishing requirements found along the way

- **Target API level.** New apps and updates must target **Android 16 (API 36)** since 31 Aug 2026 ([developer.android.com target-sdk](https://developer.android.com/google/play/requirements/target-sdk), read 2026-11-06). The Expo SDK chosen must support it.

## Part B: India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and DPDP Rules, 2025

Primary texts (downloaded from MeitY on 2026-11-06):

- **The Act.** Act No. 22 of 2023, Gazette of India Extraordinary, 11 Aug 2023. [MeitY PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)
- **The Rules.** G.S.R. 846(E), dated **13 Nov 2025** (eGazette CG-DL-E-14112025-267650). [MeitY PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)

### B1. What is in force on 2026-11-06

The Rules' own commencement clause, Rule 1:

- "(2) Rules 1, 2 and 17 to 21 shall come into force on the date of their publication". These cover the Data Protection Board, and are **in force since 13 Nov 2025**.
- "(3) Rule 4 shall come into force one year after the date of publication". This covers Consent Managers, from **about 13 Nov 2026**.
- "(4) Rules 3, 5 to 16, 22 and 23 shall come into force eighteen months after the date of publication". These hold notice, security, breach, retention, children, rights and cross-border rules, from **about 13 May 2027**.

The Act's own sections commence under G.S.R. 843(E) of the same date. I did not obtain the gazette text of 843(E); the following comes from secondary reporting ([taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/govt-notifies-commencement-dates-for-dpdp-act/), [Mondaq](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/india/privacy-protection/1710998/notification-of-digital-personal-data-protection-law-in-india-certain-key-timelines)):

- sections 1(2), 2 and 18–26 (the Board) commenced immediately
- sections 6(9) and 27(1)(d) (Consent Managers) commence after one year
- the substantive duties (sections 3–17 apart from 6(9), and penalties) commence after **18 months**

**Proposal to shorten the timeline.** In Jan 2026 MeitY consulted on cutting the 18 months to 12 (feedback closed 4 Feb 2026). That would move the main duties to **about 13 Nov 2026** ([Business Standard, Jan 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/technology/tech-news/meity-may-cut-compliance-timeline-for-key-dpdp-rules-to-12-months-126012201293_1.html)). Reports conflict on whether the proposal covered every Data Fiduciary or only Significant Data Fiduciaries. A law-firm tracker reviewed on 20 Sep 2026 found **no amending notification** ([MirvoLegal](https://www.mirvolegal.com/insights/dpdp-rules-2025-compliance-timeline/)).

**Open:** check egazette.gov.in for an amendment before relying on the May 2027 date. Either way the gap is small, so **design as if every duty below applies at launch**.

**Penalties** (Act, Schedule):

- up to ₹250 crore for failing to keep reasonable security safeguards
- up to ₹200 crore each for breach-notice failures and for breaching the children's duties
- up to ₹50 crore for anything else

### B2. Who WearAI is under the Act

- The developer is the **Data Fiduciary**. Every User is a **Data Principal**. Hosted model APIs, cloud storage and databases are **Data Processors**.
- The Act applies to processing outside India too, when it is "in connection with any activity related to offering of goods or services to Data Principals within the territory of India" (s.3(b)).
- "A Data Fiduciary may engage ... a Data Processor ... only under a valid contract" (s.8(2)). That contract must include "appropriate provision ... for taking reasonable security safeguards" (Rule 6(1)(f)).
  - The model providers' and cloud providers' standard terms or DPAs need to be in place and kept on file.
- WearAI is **not** a Significant Data Fiduciary unless the government notifies it as one (s.10). SDF duties (Data Protection Officer, audits, DPIA, possible data localisation under Rule 13(4)) don't apply.
- The DPDP Act has no "sensitive data" class. Body photos are ordinary personal data under it, though breaching them would carry a high risk.

### B3. Notice and consent

- **Consent standard.** Consent must be "free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous with a clear affirmative action", and "limited to such personal data as is necessary for such specified purpose" (s.6(1)).
  - Don't bundle consent for things the service doesn't need, such as analytics or marketing, with consent for the core service. Ask for them separately.
- **What the notice must contain.** Every consent request must be "accompanied or preceded by a notice" giving the personal data and purpose, how to withdraw and exercise rights, and how to complain to the Board (s.5(1)). Rule 3 adds that the notice must:
  - "be presented and be understandable independently of any other information", so it can't just be a link to the privacy policy
  - give "an itemised description of such personal data", plus "the specified purpose ... and specific description of the goods or services"
  - give a link to the website or app and a description of how to withdraw consent, exercise rights and complain to the Board
- **Language.** The Data Principal must have "the option to access" the notice and the consent request "in English or any language specified in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution" (s.5(3), s.6(3)). The consent request must also give the contact details of the person who handles rights requests (s.6(3)).
  - **Open:** read most strictly, every one of the 22 Eighth-Schedule languages must be offered on request. Ask counsel how far an English-plus-Hindi MVP falls short.
- **Withdrawal.** The User can withdraw consent "at any time, with the ease of doing so being comparable to the ease with which such consent was given" (s.6(4)). A one-tap consent therefore needs a one-tap withdrawal in Settings.
  - After withdrawal, the Fiduciary must "within a reasonable time, cease and cause its Data Processors to cease processing" (s.6(6)) and erase the data (s.8(7)).
- **Consent Managers** (Rule 4, from about Nov 2026) are optional platforms for Data Principals. Nothing in the text requires WearAI to integrate one.

### B4. Rights, grievances, contact person

- **Rights.** Users have the right to:
  - access a summary of their personal data and processing (s.11)
  - correct, complete, update and erase it (s.12)
  - grievance redressal (s.13)
  - nominate someone to act on their behalf on death or incapacity (s.14)
- **Publish how to make a request.** Prominently publish "the details of the means using which a Data Principal may make a request", and the identifier needed, which for WearAI is the Google account email (Rule 14(1)).
- **Grievances.** Publish and keep to a grievance response time "not exceeding ninety days" (Rule 14(3)).
- **Contact person.** Prominently publish on the website or app, and put in every reply to a rights request, "the business contact information of ... a person who is able to answer" questions about the processing (Rule 9, s.8(9)).

### B5. Children (anyone under 18)

- "'child' means an individual who has not completed the age of eighteen years" (s.2(f)).
- **Verifiable parental consent** is required "before processing any personal data of a child" (s.9(1)). Rule 10 says how: check that the parent is an identifiable adult, using reliable identity and age details the Fiduciary already holds, or details or a virtual token from an authorised entity such as DigiLocker.
- **Further bans.** Processing "likely to cause any detrimental effect on the well-being of a child" is banned (s.9(2)), as are "tracking or behavioural monitoring of children" (s.9(3)). The Feed's use of dismissals to shape later Feeds is behavioural.
- **What this means for WearAI.** The Act doesn't spell out how to verify age. Because WearAI won't build a parental-consent flow, any processing of a minor's data is a s.9 breach (up to ₹200 crore). A defensible MVP stack, with every layer recorded in the spec:
  1. Play Console set to 18+ only, with Restrict Minor Access (A6).
  2. An in-app age gate before any photo is taken: date of birth or an explicit 18+ declaration, with a refusal path.
  3. In the Terms, an 18+ condition and a statement that photos must be of the User only.
  4. A process: if a User is found to be under 18 (a report, or a later Play Age Signals result), stop processing and erase their data.
- **Open:** whether an age gate the User self-declares is enough "due diligence" in India. Ask counsel. The Rules give no standard for screening users out as adults, only for verifying parents.

### B6. Security, breaches, retention

- **Minimum safeguards** (Rule 6(1)):
  - encryption, masking or tokenisation
  - access control
  - "appropriate logs, monitoring and review"
  - backups for continuity
  - **keep logs and personal data for one year** to detect and investigate unauthorised access (Rule 6(1)(e))
  - safeguard clauses in contracts with Data Processors
- **Breach notices** (Rule 7):
  - To **each affected User**, "without delay", through their user account or a registered contact. The notice gives the nature, extent and timing of the breach, its likely consequences, what WearAI is doing, what the User can do, and a contact.
  - To **the Board**, first "without delay", then a detailed report "within seventy-two hours".
- **Erasure** (s.8(7)). Erase personal data when the User withdraws consent, or once "the specified purpose is no longer being served", whichever comes first, and make Data Processors erase too.
  - The fixed inactivity deadlines in the Third Schedule (3 years, with a 48-hour warning) apply only to e-commerce, online-gaming and social-media intermediaries with ≥2 crore registered users in India. WearAI is none of these.
- **The 1-year retention minimum.** "a Data Fiduciary shall retain ... such personal data, associated traffic data and other logs of the processing for a minimum period of one year from the date of such processing" for Seventh Schedule purposes (state and law-enforcement requests) (Rule 8(3)). The Rule's own illustration says this applies "even if X deletes her account".
  - **Open (important):** this collides with deleting a User's account and data under Play's rules (A5). Does "such personal data" cover the Reference Photos themselves, or only processing records and logs? Get a legal reading. Whatever the answer, put the retention in the privacy policy and on the Play deletion page as a legal-compliance exception, which Play allows. One possible design: on deletion, erase photos and generated images at once, and keep a minimal processing log, access-controlled and encrypted, for 12 months.

### B7. Storing data outside India

- "The Central Government may, by notification, restrict the transfer of personal data ... to such country or territory outside India as may be so notified" (s.16(1)). This is a **negative list**. Transfers are allowed unless a country is restricted, and **no country had been notified as of Sept 2026** (secondary: [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/columns/the-dpdp-cross-border-transfer-rules-arent-live-yet-so-why-are-contracts-being-redrafted-as-if-they-are), [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1764976/from-localisation-debates-to-a-negative-list-making-cross-border-data-transfers-work-under-indias-dpdp-act)).
- Transfers abroad are subject to any requirements the government sets "in respect of making such personal data available to any foreign State" (Rule 15, from about May 2027).
- Data localisation (Rule 13(4)) binds only Significant Data Fiduciaries.
- **What this means for WearAI.** Hosted model APIs and cloud regions outside India are lawful today. Keep a list of processing locations and a way to switch region, in case a destination is later restricted. Hosting in India (e.g. a Mumbai region) is optional.

## Checklist: what the spec must include

**Content safety (Play)**

- [ ] Input and output filters on generation. No nudity, sexual or suggestive poses, sheer clothing or lingerie. Never any undressing. Only the User's own photos as subjects.
- [ ] An in-app "Report" action on every generated image (Feed Post and Style Me), sending reports to a moderation queue that feeds back into the filters.
- [ ] A record of red-team or safety testing done before launch (SAIF / OWASP GenAI).

**Permissions (Play)**

- [ ] The Android photo picker only. A merged-manifest check that no media or storage permission is present.

**Disclosure, consent and policies (Play + DPDP)**

- [ ] An onboarding notice and consent screen before the first Reference Photo. It must stand alone, list every item of personal data and each purpose, name third-party AI processing, show how to withdraw consent and exercise rights, and link to the Board. Consent by affirmative action.
- [ ] Separate opt-ins for anything optional, such as analytics.
- [ ] Notice and consent screens available in English plus Indian languages (scope **Open** for counsel).
- [ ] Withdraw consent in one tap in Settings. On withdrawal: stop processing, erase the data, and tell Data Processors to do the same.
- [ ] A privacy policy at a public, non-PDF URL, linked in the Play Console and in the app. It covers the data, purposes, recipients and processors, retention (including the 1-year log retention), deletion, the contact person, the grievance process and the 90-day response time, and Board complaints.
- [ ] Data safety form: Photos, Personal info, App activity; service providers not "shared" (requires model API tiers that don't train on our data); encrypted in transit; deletion offered.

**User rights and deletion (Play + DPDP)**

- [ ] Delete the account in the app and through a web page. This erases photos, generated images, the Wardrobe, Posts and conversations from our systems and from processors, on a stated timeline. Documented exceptions for legal retention.
- [ ] Rights requests (access, correct, erase, nominate) and a grievance channel. A published contact person. Responses within 90 days.

**18+ (Play + DPDP)**

- [ ] Play target audience 18+ only, with Restrict Minor Access turned on.
- [ ] An in-app age gate before any photo is collected, plus an 18+ clause in the Terms.
- [ ] A process to cut off and erase any account found to belong to someone under 18.

**Security and breaches (DPDP)**

- [ ] Encryption at rest and in transit, least-privilege access, access logs kept for 1 year, backups.
- [ ] A breach runbook: notify affected Users through the account or email without delay, and the Board within 72 hours.
- [ ] Contracts or DPAs with every Data Processor, including security clauses.
- [ ] A list of processing locations, with the ability to change region.

**Publishing (Play)**

- [ ] Target API 36.
- [ ] Choose the account type. A personal account means a closed test of ≥12 testers for 14 continuous days, then the production-access application. Allow about 3 weeks in the launch plan.

## Out of scope here, but likely relevant

- **India's IT (Intermediary Guidelines) Amendment Rules, 2026 on "synthetically generated information"**: notified 10 Feb 2026, in force 20 Feb 2026 (secondary: [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/india-introduces-mandatory-labelling-for-ai-and-3hour-takedown-for-illegal-content)). According to Hogan Lovells:
  - Intermediaries offering tools that create synthetic media must label output prominently, embed permanent metadata or provenance markers, block unlawful synthetic media such as non-consensual intimate imagery, act on orders within 3 hours, and warn users every 3 months.
  - Whether WearAI, which makes private images of the User, is an intermediary in scope was not researched. **Suggest a new ticket.**
- Content rating (IARC questionnaire) and store-listing metadata rules were not researched in detail.
